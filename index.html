The First 30 Days After a Serious Idaho Crash

The first month is easier to manage when urgent care, evidence, and administration are separated into short intervals.

Educational scope. This page provides general information, not legal or medical advice. Laws, policies, deadlines, and outcomes depend on specific facts.

Hours 0–24: health and immediate facts

Obtain emergency help when needed, follow discharge instructions, and avoid unsafe scene activity. If conditions permit, exchange insurance and contact information, identify witnesses, photograph the scene and vehicles, and record the responding agency’s case number. Notify a trusted person where the vehicle was towed and keep damaged safety equipment or personal property rather than discarding it casually.

Days 2–3: create the master file

Start a simple index with sections for official reports, photographs, insurance, medical care, employment, expenses, and correspondence. Write a factual account while memory is fresh, clearly marking estimates and unknowns. Report the loss under applicable policies and save confirmation numbers. Calendar follow-up care and note any work restrictions.

Week 1: verify continuity

Confirm that referrals were received, prescriptions are understood, and transportation is available. Ask the tow yard about access and storage charges. Identify potential camera sources before footage cycles out. Order or calendar the crash report. Review every request from an insurer and respond accurately, but distinguish a request for basic notice from a broad authorization or final release.

Weeks 2–3: reconcile the records

Compare the medical visit list with bills, the claim contact list with email, and the expense log with receipts. Correct factual errors through the appropriate record process rather than silently editing copies. Ask the adjuster what additional material is needed and when liability or vehicle value is expected to be reviewed. Keep the property claim moving without assuming it controls the injury timetable.

Week 4: assess what remains uncertain

By day 30, some injuries are still evolving and some investigations are incomplete. List open issues: diagnosis, prognosis, work capacity, vehicle disposition, coverage, fault, missing records, and future appointments. Idaho Code contains time limits for injury actions, often discussed as a two-year period, but accrual rules and exceptions are legal questions; obtaining individualized advice early avoids last-minute assumptions.

Create a recurring weekly review

Choose one time each week to reconcile appointments, claim messages, expenses, work restrictions, and unresolved requests. This keeps administration from consuming every day and makes omissions visible early. The review should produce a short next-action list with an owner and date. Close completed items rather than letting the log become an unfiltered diary.

Protect personal information

Crash claims can require sensitive documents, but a request should still be examined for scope, date range, recipient, and purpose. Use secure delivery methods offered by the insurer or provider, redact unrelated identifiers when appropriate, and keep a copy of every authorization signed. Broad medical, employment, or tax authorizations can have consequences beyond a single missing record, so individualized advice may be appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best first organizational step?

Create one master index linking claim numbers, providers, documents, dates, and unresolved tasks.

Should a vehicle be released immediately?

Routine property claims may move quickly, but a severe crash can warrant advice about inspection and preservation before alteration or disposal.

Is the Idaho injury deadline always exactly two years?

Idaho Code § 5-219 contains a two-year limitation for many personal-injury actions, but the correct deadline depends on the claim and facts.
